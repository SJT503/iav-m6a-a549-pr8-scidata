### [P5] Abstract — replace
N6-methyladenosine (m6A) is among the most prevalent internal modifications of eukaryotic mRNA and shapes host responses to viral infection, yet transcriptome-wide maps of how influenza A virus remodels the host m6A landscape remain limited. We report an m6A epitranscriptomic microarray dataset of human A549 alveolar epithelial cells infected with influenza A virus PR8 (H1N1) at a multiplicity of infection of 0.5 for 24 hours, or mock-infected, with three biological replicates per condition. Methylated RNA immunoprecipitation was combined with two-colour hybridisation on the Arraystar Human mRNA&lncRNA Epitranscriptomic Microarray (8×60K), which measures for each transcript the m6A quantity (immunoprecipitated, Cy5 signal), the expression level (supernatant, Cy3 signal) and %modified (the immunoprecipitated share of total signal). The dataset provides raw and normalised intensities for 40,955 probes across six samples, methylation and expression profiles for coding and non-coding RNAs, and quality-control records for each sample. It is a resource for studying virus-induced epitranscriptomic changes and for generating hypotheses about m6A regulation during influenza infection.

### [P8] Background & Summary, paragraph 2 — replace the final two sentences ("For influenza A virus (IAV) specifically ... remain scarce [23].")
For influenza A virus (IAV), m6A on viral RNA enhances viral gene expression and replication [ref:courtney2017]. The m6A reader YTHDC1, whose levels increase during infection, binds a 3′ splice site of the NS segment and inhibits its splicing [ref:zhu2023]; m6A at a conserved site in NS mRNA recruits YTHDC1, which competes with the splicing factor SRSF3, so that NS1 regulates the splicing of its own mRNA [ref:liao2025]; and m6A on vRNA promotes polymerase activity and the assembly of viral ribonucleoprotein complexes [ref:wang2025]. On the host side, the kinase TBK1 phosphorylates METTL3, m6A stabilises IRF3 mRNA, and mice lacking METTL3 in myeloid cells are more susceptible to lethal IAV infection [ref:chen2022]. Yet transcriptome-wide datasets that capture the paired host m6A and expression response to IAV in a well-characterised cell model, and that are openly deposited for reuse, remain scarce [ref:liu2024].

### [P9] Background & Summary, paragraph 3 — replace the first sentence
Seasonal influenza causes an estimated 290,000–650,000 respiratory deaths annually [ref:iuliano2018] and influenza A viruses continue to pose a pandemic threat as seasonal strains circulate and novel variants emerge [ref:moriyama2020].

### [P11] Background & Summary, paragraph 5 — replace
The released dataset comprises raw and normalised intensities for 40,955 probes across the six samples, m6A-quantity and expression profiles for mRNAs, lncRNAs and other non-coding RNAs, and the quality-control records of each sample. Because each condition was profiled in three biological replicates, the data support assessment of within-group reproducibility and comparison between conditions. The dataset is intended as a descriptive epitranscriptomic resource and a basis for hypothesis generation about m6A regulation during influenza infection, rather than as evidence for a specific regulatory mechanism. It can be used to identify candidate m6A-regulated transcripts in the infected respiratory epithelium, to study the behaviour of m6A regulatory enzymes during infection, and to benchmark or integrate with other openly released RNA-modification datasets generated in human cells [ref:pinello2024,ref:chen2024sd].

### [P15] Methods, "Cells and virus" — replace
Human lung carcinoma A549 cells (ATCC, CCL-185) and Madin–Darby canine kidney (MDCK) cells (ATCC, CCL-34) were authenticated by short tandem repeat profiling and tested negative for mycoplasma contamination by Guangzhou IGE Biotechnology Co., Ltd. (Guangzhou, China) in 2021, before the infection experiments. A549 cells were maintained in Dulbecco's modified Eagle medium (DMEM) supplemented with 10% fetal bovine serum and 1% penicillin–streptomycin at 37 °C in a humidified 5% CO₂ incubator. Influenza A virus strain PR8 (A/Puerto Rico/8/1934, H1N1), supplied by Prof. Meilin Jin (Huazhong Agricultural University, China), was amplified in approximately 10-day-old embryonated chicken eggs by inoculation into the allantoic cavity, incubated at 37 °C for 72 h, and harvested from the allantoic fluid. The harvested virus was clarified by low-speed centrifugation (2,000 rpm, 3 min), aliquoted, and stored at −80 °C. Infectious titres were determined by plaque assay on MDCK cells (10-fold serial dilutions, 1% agarose overlay, 48–72 h incubation); the stock used for infection contained 3 × 10⁷ plaque-forming units (PFU) per mL. A549 cells were seeded in six-well plates at 1 × 10⁶ cells per well and inoculated with 17 µL of virus stock per well (5.1 × 10⁵ PFU), giving a multiplicity of infection (MOI) of 0.5 PFU per cell calculated on the number of cells seeded (3 × 10⁷ PFU mL⁻¹ × 0.017 mL ÷ 1 × 10⁶ cells = 0.51). Mock-infected cells received the same volume of infection medium without virus. Cells were exposed to virus for 1 h at 37 °C in infection medium containing 1 µg mL⁻¹ TPCK-trypsin and then incubated in DMEM supplemented with 0.3% BSA and 1 µg mL⁻¹ TPCK-trypsin. A cytopathic effect was observed in PR8-infected wells at 24 h, when cells were harvested. Each of the three biological replicates per condition came from a different cell passage and was infected on a different day; mock and PR8 replicates were not processed as matched pairs. This gave six samples (MOCK_1, MOCK_2, MOCK_3, PR8_1, PR8_2 and PR8_3).

### [P19] Methods, "m6A methylated RNA immunoprecipitation (MeRIP)" — replace
Immunoprecipitation was performed by the service provider (Aksomics, Shanghai, China) according to its standard protocol. For each sample, 3–5 µg of total RNA was mixed with an m6A spike-in control mixture containing an m6A-positive (methylated) and an m6A-negative (unmethylated) control RNA; the exact amount used in each reaction is not recorded in the delivered documentation. The mixture was incubated in 300 µL IP buffer (50 mM Tris-HCl pH 7.4, 150 mM NaCl, 0.1% NP-40) supplemented with RNase inhibitor (Enzymatics, Y9240L; supplied at 40 U µL⁻¹), containing 2 µg of affinity-purified anti-N6-methyladenosine rabbit polyclonal antibody (Synaptic Systems, 202003), with head-over-tail rotation at 4 °C for 2 h. Dynabeads™ M-280 Sheep Anti-Rabbit IgG (Invitrogen, 11203D; 20 µL per sample) were blocked with 0.5% BSA at 4 °C for 2 h, washed three times with IP buffer, and resuspended in the RNA–antibody mixture; binding was carried out with rotation at 4 °C for 2 h. Beads were washed three times with IP buffer and twice with wash buffer (50 mM Tris-HCl pH 7.4, 50 mM NaCl, 0.1% NP-40, with the same RNase inhibitor). The m6A-enriched RNA was eluted with elution buffer (10 mM Tris-HCl pH 7.4, 1 mM EDTA, 0.05% SDS, 40 U Proteinase K, 1 µL RNase inhibitor) at 50 °C for 1 h. The immunoprecipitated RNA ("IP") and the RNA recovered from the supernatant (non-immunoprecipitated fraction; "Sup") were extracted with acid phenol–chloroform and ethanol-precipitated. Recovery of the two spike-in RNAs in the IP and Sup fractions was measured by qPCR (primer sets CTA850 for the positive and CTA650 for the negative control).

### [P21] Methods, "Labelling and microarray hybridisation" — replace
The IP and Sup RNAs were each combined with an equal amount of a separate calibration spike-in control RNA, amplified, and labelled with Cy5 (IP) and Cy3 (Sup) using the Arraystar Super RNA Labeling Kit (Arraystar, AL-SE-005), with 1 µg RNA per labelling reaction. The cRNAs were purified with the RNeasy Mini Kit (QIAGEN, 74105), and their concentration and specific activity (pmol dye per µg cRNA) were measured on a NanoDrop ND-1000. For each sample, 2.5 µg of Cy3- and Cy5-labelled cRNA were mixed, fragmented, combined with hybridisation buffer, and 50 µL of hybridisation solution was applied to one array of an Arraystar Human mRNA&lncRNA Epitranscriptomic Microarray (8×60K; Agilent design 085753). The six samples occupied positions 1_1–1_4 (MOCK_1, MOCK_2, MOCK_3, PR8_1) and 2_1–2_2 (PR8_2, PR8_3) on two slides; positions were assigned by the provider. Slides were hybridised at 65 °C for 17 h in an Agilent hybridisation oven, washed, fixed, and scanned in two colour channels on an Agilent Scanner G2505C on 11 November 2021.

### [P23] Methods — replace
Array images were analysed with Agilent Feature Extraction software (version 11.0.1.1), which applies within-array linear and LOWESS dye normalisation. The service provider then normalised the log2 Feature Extraction signals of each channel on each array to the average log2 intensity of the calibration spike-in RNAs; this step adds one constant to all probes of a channel on an array. A probe was retained if its IP (Cy5) feature was flagged as present or marginal on at least three of the six arrays, a rule that does not depend on condition, so that transcripts detected in only one condition are kept. This gave 40,955 of the 60,491 transcript probes on the array. Each probe corresponds to one transcript. For each probe and array, the m6A quantity is the normalised IP signal, the expression level is the normalised Sup signal, and %modified is 100 × IP/(IP + Sup), calculated on the linear scale (100 × 2^IP/(2^IP + 2^Sup) for the log2 values in Matrix.xlsx); the provider tables give the same quantity as a fraction between 0 and 1. Normalisation, probe filtering and the differential tables in the provider files were produced by the service provider. All analyses reported below were performed by the authors on the deposited files and are reproduced by the released code.

### [P24] Methods — replace (becomes a new subsection with heading "Differential analysis")
Differences between PR8 and mock were tested with limma [ref:ritchie2015] (lmFit and eBayes; two-group design with mock as reference) on the deposited probe matrix, separately for the m6A quantity (log2 IP), the expression level (log2 Sup) and %modified. %modified was analysed on the logit scale, because it is a bounded proportion, and only for the 25,279 probes whose Sup signal was well above background (Feature Extraction gIsWellAboveBG = 1) on all six arrays, because the ratio is undefined when its denominator is at background. P values were adjusted with the Benjamini–Hochberg method [ref:bh1995], over all 40,955 probes for the m6A quantity and expression and over the 25,279 tested probes for %modified. A probe was called differential at FDR < 0.05 and |fold change| ≥ 2 (m6A quantity and expression) or |Δ%modified| ≥ 5 percentage points (%modified). To evaluate the sensitivity of %modified to the array-level normalisation constants, the analysis was repeated after joint quantile normalisation of the twelve IP and Sup channels [ref:bolstad2003] and after subtracting each array's median log2 IP/Sup ratio from its IP intensities. Principal component analysis used prcomp in R on all 40,955 probes (mean-centred, not scaled). Results are reported per probe, labelled with transcript identifier and gene symbol, without collapsing probes to genes. An overview of the experimental design and data workflow is shown in Figure 1.

### [NEW] Technical Validation, "Infection response"
To assess whether the infected cultures mounted an antiviral response, we examined interferon and interferon-stimulated transcripts in the expression layer (Table 4). IFNL1, IFNB1, CCL5 and RSAD2 increased 63- to about 2,700-fold in PR8-infected cells (log2 fold change 5.98–11.39), and ISG15, PML, IFNL3, IFIT3, OAS2, OAS1, OASL and MX2 increased 8.5- to 54-fold (FDR ≤ 0.0012 for all twelve). Together with the cytopathic effect observed at 24 h, this indicates a productive infection in all three PR8 cultures. The same transcripts show large gains in m6A quantity (Table 4), which largely follow their induction, because m6A quantity scales with transcript abundance.

### [P45] Technical Validation, "Methylation level versus methylation fraction" — replace
The array reports two measures of m6A for each transcript: the m6A quantity, which scales with both methylation and transcript abundance, and %modified, which is normalised for abundance. To assess whether the two measures carry distinct information, we compared the change in %modified with the change in expression for the 20,165 mRNA probes with a detectable Sup signal. Of the 3,153 mRNAs whose expression changed at least two-fold, 47% changed in %modified by less than 5 percentage points, and 1,516 mRNAs with nearly unchanged expression (|log2 fold change| < 0.5) changed in %modified by at least 10 percentage points; the two changes were almost uncorrelated (Pearson r = −0.08; Figure 4a). Because %modified depends on the array-level normalisation constants, we repeated the comparison after quantile normalisation of the IP and Sup channels, and the decoupling was preserved (52% of 2,709 mRNAs; r = −0.06; Figure 4b). In the limma analysis, 468 probes differed in %modified at FDR < 0.05 and |Δ%modified| ≥ 5 percentage points (366 higher in PR8, 102 higher in mock; Figure 4c), of which 210 were also called in the same direction after quantile normalisation (116 higher in PR8, 94 higher in mock; Supplementary Table 1). The excess of probes with higher %modified in PR8 in the deposited data reflects an array-level offset (see Usage Notes).

### [NEW after P55] Usage Notes (Compact style, as P53-P55)
Specificity of the immunoprecipitation. Selectivity of the anti-m6A pull-down was monitored with m6A-positive and m6A-negative spike-in RNAs (Table 2). The provider's quality-control plan also specified a mock immunoprecipitation with non-immune serum, but per-sample mock-IP values were not part of the delivered data and are not reported. The spike-ins show that the antibody discriminates methylated from unmethylated RNA; they do not measure non-specific binding of endogenous transcripts to the antibody–bead complex, which would add a background component to the IP channel of every probe and cannot be separated from m6A-dependent enrichment in these data. Such background affects absolute calls of whether a transcript is methylated more than comparisons between conditions, provided that non-specific binding is similar in the two conditions. Users who need absolute calls for individual transcripts should confirm them with an orthogonal assay, such as MeRIP–qPCR with an isotype-matched IgG control.

Array-level offsets in the IP-to-Sup ratio. The provider's calibration adds one constant to the log2 signals of each channel on each array (Table 3). Because every probe on an array receives the same constant, a difference between arrays in the IP-minus-Sup constant shifts %modified for all probes on that array. The IP-minus-Sup constant was on average 0.26 log2 units higher on the PR8 arrays than on the mock arrays (Welch t-test, P = 0.18), and the per-array median log2(IP/Sup) ratio tracked the recovery of the positive spike-in, a measure of immunoprecipitation efficiency (Pearson r = 0.71, n = 6, P = 0.11; r = 0.69 for the calibration constant itself). This offset accounts for most of the excess of probes with higher %modified in PR8: among the 25,279 tested probes, the median Δ%modified was +4.7 percentage points in the deposited data, +1.1 after subtracting each array's median log2 IP/Sup ratio and +1.0 after quantile normalisation. A global change in m6A stoichiometry therefore cannot be distinguished from a difference in immunoprecipitation efficiency in these data, and global shifts in %modified should not be interpreted biologically. The same constants also shift the m6A quantity (median log2 fold change PR8/mock +0.25 in the deposited data and +0.08 after quantile normalisation), although 6,286 of the 7,575 differential m6A-quantity calls (83%) made with the deposited normalisation are also made after quantile normalisation. For transcript-level comparisons of %modified, we recommend restricting the analysis to probes whose Sup signal is above background on every array compared, and removing the per-array offset or applying quantile normalisation.

Array layout. Array positions were assigned by the provider and were not randomised; the arrays were scanned in three images, one of which contained only PR8_3 (Table 3), and the design labels IP RNA with Cy5 and Sup RNA with Cy3 in every sample, without a dye-swap. Dye effects are therefore confounded with the IP/Sup comparison, and scan image is not fully balanced across conditions.

Direction of the provider tables. The provider tables use fold change = mock/PR8. For example, IFNB1 (ENST00000380232) appears in the sheet hypo_MOCK_vs_PR8 with a fold change of 0.00093, a 1,070-fold increase in PR8. The probe filter retains probes detected in only one condition; their fold changes compare a detected signal with background and should be read as lower bounds.
